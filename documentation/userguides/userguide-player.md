# Player Guide

**Audience:** players in a game that uses Herald.

What Herald does and does not do to your game. Short, because the honest answer is: almost nothing.

## Herald does not change your client

Herald configures exactly one client — the dedicated camera user your GM set up for streaming or a room display. Your own Foundry looks and behaves exactly as it would without Herald installed. Your interface is not hidden, your camera is not moved, and nothing is taken away from you.

If your view is behaving oddly, Herald is not the cause.

## What the camera is

Your GM has logged in an extra user whose only job is to show the table. It has no character and takes no turns. It sits on the map with Foundry's interface hidden and follows the action automatically, so the group can watch on a TV or an audience can watch a stream.

You may see it in the players list as an extra name, and it will show as online. That is expected.

## When the camera follows you

Two of the GM's camera modes involve you specifically:

- **Mirror** puts the camera on *your* viewport. What you look at, the audience sees. If your GM tells you they are mirroring your view, be aware that panning around the map or opening a sheet is visible to everyone watching.
- **Follow** locks the camera onto a single token, which may be yours. This tracks the token, not your screen — what you look at stays private.

Your GM chooses these; you cannot set them yourself, and there is no indicator on your screen telling you which is active. If it matters to you, ask.

## What the camera can see

The camera sees the world with the permissions of its own user. If your GM set it up as a normal player, it sees what a player sees — fog you have not explored stays dark, hidden tokens stay hidden. If they set it up as a GM, it sees everything.

This is worth knowing if your table streams publicly: ask your GM which they used.

## Something looks wrong

Herald problems appear on the camera, not on your client. If your own Foundry is misbehaving, the cause is elsewhere. If the *stream* looks wrong — interface showing, camera not moving, a window stuck on screen — tell your GM, who can fix it from the View Mode menu.
